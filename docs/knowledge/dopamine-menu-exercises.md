# Dopamine Menu — Exercise Catalog

## Exercise: 5-Minute Shake-Out
Target feeling: anxious, restless, overwhelmed
Duration: 5 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Stand up and shake out your arms, legs, and whole body for 30 seconds. Repeat 3 times with 10-second rests.
Why it helps: Physical shaking releases built-up tension and activates the parasympathetic nervous system, helping your body reset.

## Exercise: Box Breathing
Target feeling: anxious, stressed, can't focus
Duration: 4 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Inhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds, exhale for 4 seconds, hold for 4 seconds. Repeat for 4 minutes.
Why it helps: Box breathing slows your heart rate and signals safety to your nervous system, reducing anxiety and improving focus.

## Exercise: Power Pose Hold
Target feeling: low confidence, unmotivated, sluggish
Duration: 2 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Stand with feet shoulder-width apart, hands on hips, chest open, chin slightly up. Hold for 2 minutes while breathing slowly.
Why it helps: Expansive postures can temporarily boost confidence and energy by reducing cortisol and increasing feelings of presence.

## Exercise: Brisk 10-Minute Walk
Target feeling: sluggish, low energy, brain fog
Duration: 10 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Walk at a brisk pace outdoors or around your home. Focus on your surroundings and breathing.
Why it helps: Moderate cardio increases blood flow to the brain and triggers dopamine release, lifting mood and sharpening focus.

## Exercise: Dance It Out
Target feeling: stressed, tense, need a mood shift
Duration: 5 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Put on your favorite song and move freely — no choreography needed. Let your body lead.
Why it helps: Freestyle movement to music combines cardio, rhythm, and joy — a potent dopamine boost that also releases emotional tension.

## Exercise: Progressive Muscle Relaxation
Target feeling: tense, anxious, can't sleep
Duration: 10 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Starting from your feet, tense each muscle group for 5 seconds, then release. Work your way up your body.
Why it helps: Systematically releasing muscle tension signals your nervous system that it's safe to relax, making it easier to unwind.

## Exercise: Cold Water Splash
Target feeling: overwhelmed, panicked, dissociating
Duration: 1 minute
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Splash cold water on your face or hold an ice cube in your hand for 30–60 seconds.
Why it helps: Cold exposure activates the dive reflex, which slows your heart rate and brings you back into your body quickly.

## Exercise: Stretch & Breathe
Target feeling: stiff, tense, been sitting too long
Duration: 5 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Do 5 gentle stretches (neck rolls, shoulder shrugs, side bends, forward fold, chest opener). Hold each for 30 seconds with slow breathing.
Why it helps: Stretching releases physical tension and improves circulation, while deep breathing calms the nervous system.

## Exercise: Gratitude Body Scan
Target feeling: low mood, disconnected, hard on yourself
Duration: 5 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Close your eyes and slowly scan from your head to your toes. For each body part, think of one thing you're grateful your body lets you do.
Why it helps: Combining body awareness with gratitude shifts your brain toward positive associations, gently lifting mood.

## Exercise: Jumping Jacks Burst
Target feeling: sluggish, need quick energy, procrastinating
Duration: 2 minutes
Difficulty: easy
Instructions: Do jumping jacks for 2 minutes straight. If needed, break into 30-second intervals with 10-second rests.
Why it helps: Quick bursts of cardio flood your brain with oxygen and dopamine, breaking through inertia and jumpstarting motivation.
